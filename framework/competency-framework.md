# AI Visual Competency Framework v0.3 — UNESCO-Aligned Adaptation

**Status:** Draft for pilot, not a validated standard. **Updated:** 2026-10-21. **Audience:** designers learning AI-assisted visual creation.

## Attribution and scope

This framework **adapts the progression** *Understand → Apply → Create* from UNESCO's [AI Competency Framework for Students (2024)](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ai-competency-framework-students) (SRC-001). UNESCO's original audience is **school students**, and its framework comprises **12 competencies in four dimensions**. The Hub does **not** reproduce those 12 competencies as seven design skills; it **crosswalks** the four dimensions to its own visual-creation objectives. Neither endorsement nor accreditation by UNESCO is implied.

**Do not conflate:** the three UNESCO progression levels with a proven, linear learning sequence or a validated measurement scale. A learner may demonstrate Create in one domain and Understand in another. "Create" means **autonomous synthesis and justification**, not merely clicking Generate.

## UNESCO reference dimensions and adaptation boundary

| UNESCO dimension (original, translated) | Original focus | What the Hub inherits | What the Hub adds/changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Human-centred mindset | Human agency and human–AI relations | Human decision-making and agency | Designer ownership of briefs, art direction and acceptance criteria |
| Ethics of AI | Ethics, responsible choices | Awareness, reflection and responsible action | Image consent, provenance, rights awareness and disclosure tasks |
| AI techniques and applications | AI knowledge, applications and critical use | Understanding capabilities, limits and application | Image-model-specific composition, character, lighting, reference and editing practice |
| AI system design | Design-oriented understanding and creation of AI systems | Iterative design and problem solving as an analogy | Creative workflow design, **not** claims that learners engineer AI systems |

**Important:** this is a **dimension-level conceptual crosswalk**, not an exact mapping of UNESCO's 12 individual competencies. Detailed competency-by-competency UNESCO mapping requires direct review of the full original framework text and is an open research task.

## Proficiency levels — observable interpretation

| Level | Learner evidence | Assessment boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Understand** | Explain a concept, identify constraints, compare examples, recognize limitations | Can explain **why**, without necessarily producing a successful final image |
| **Apply** | Execute a defined brief using a tool, inspect results and correct deviations | Reaches specified observable criteria with documented assistance |
| **Create** | Independently define direction, combine skills, iterate, justify trade-offs and deliver a defensible result | Demonstrates transfer to a new brief; no promise of perfect output |

These operational definitions are authored by the Hub; UNESCO supplies the level names and broad educational progression, not the exact visual rubrics.

### Why these are not one-to-one UNESCO competencies
UNESCO's 12 competencies describe broad AI literacy for students. The Hub's AV-02/03/04/05 split a **single broad application domain** into practical designer specializations. Conversely, UNESCO's ethics and human agency appear across **multiple** Hub competencies. The mapping is many-to-many and interpretive, not a claimed direct equivalence.

## Assessment criteria (rubric anchors for each level)

Use **0 = not demonstrated, 1 = partially demonstrated, 2 = demonstrated** per observable criterion; record task, model/version, assessor, assistance, date and artifacts. Scores are **formative**, not validated certifications.

| Hub | Understand criterion | Apply criterion | Create criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| AV-01 | Correctly labels ≥4 brief elements | Produces brief with audience, purpose, ≥3 constraints, acceptance test | Defends choices and resolves conflicting constraints in a new brief |
| AV-02 | Identifies subject hierarchy, framing and negative space | Meets subject placement and text-safe-area constraints in a supplied task | Explains a novel composition with trade-offs and revision evidence |
| AV-03 | Lists subject count, pose and interaction requirements | Output meets task-specific count and readable-pose checks | Independently coordinates distinct roles/poses in a new scenario |
| AV-04 | Distinguishes light direction, contrast and material cues | Matches a defined lighting/style checklist | Maintains coherent treatment across ≥2 related assets and justifies deviations |
| AV-05 | Explains reference versus copy/edit and limitations | Performs an edit and documents what changed versus stayed | Designs a rights-aware reference-to-edit sequence and logs failures |
| AV-06 | Names ≥2 observable mismatches and priorities | Demonstrates targeted before/after revision with unchanged constraints | Transfers critique protocol to a new brief and justifies stopping point |
| AV-08 | Identifies relevant consent, provenance and risk issues | Records inputs, permissions/uncertainties and disclosure decision | Produces and justifies a risk-handling plan for a realistic scenario |
| AV-07 | Recognizes deliverable specs | Checks size, format and editability as specified | Packages assets and explains production limitations (provisional) |

Thresholds (such as ≥4, ≥3 and ≥2) are **proposed pilot anchors**, not UNESCO thresholds or empirically calibrated cut scores. A criterion may be marked "not assessable" when a platform cannot expose necessary evidence.

## Worked example — AV-02 Composition

- **Understand:** compare two images; annotate visual hierarchy, camera angle and headline-safe space. Evidence: annotated comparison and explanation.
- **Apply:** using a provided 4:5 brief, produce a five-person editorial composition with distinct poses and a designated text-safe region; revise once. Evidence: brief, prompt/settings, before/after images and checklist.
- **Create:** given a new brand brief with no sample layout, propose two directions, choose one, generate and critique, and justify visual trade-offs. Evidence: concept rationale, artifacts, revision log, final handoff.

**Suggested scoring:** three criteria per stage, each 0–2. Do not claim a level is mastered on the basis of a single successful image. Repeat tasks across briefs and assess inter-rater agreement in the pilot.

## Guardrails and next validation

1. Cite UNESCO as **inspiration for progression and AI-literacy dimensions**, not as certifier of Hub skills.
2. Do not rename UNESCO's 12 competencies as the Hub's seven or eight.
3. No universal prompt recipe, cross-model performance guarantee or automated scoring claims.
4. Validate each rubric with real designers, repeat tasks and independent raters; record failures.
5. Complete UNESCO's original **12-item** crosswalk from full text before publishing any claim of item-level alignment.
6. Review visual design theory and production standards before promoting AV-07 to core.
